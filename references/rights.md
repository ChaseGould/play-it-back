# Rights check

Do this at the interview, before any credits are spent. A blocked
asset found after the edit wastes the whole project (this happened: a
finished music clip could not ship because the song was made on an AI
music service's free plan, and upgrading later did not cover songs
already made).

Ask about every supplied or planned asset:

| Asset | Ask | Usually fine | Red flags |
|---|---|---|---|
| Song | Who made it? Which service and plan? Who owns the master and the lyrics? | The user's own recording; a paid-plan AI song whose terms grant commercial use; a licensed track with a license covering video | A commercial artist's song; AI music made on a free tier; "I found it on YouTube" |
| Background music | Where from? | YouTube Audio Library, Pixabay Music, or a paid stock license whose terms cover this use; music made locally in code | Any copyrighted track, even a few seconds |
| Photos | Who took them? Who or what is in them? | The user's own photos of their own things | Other people's faces or houses without consent; readable license plates and house numbers (blur them) |
| Logos and brand assets | Is this the user's brand, or a client's with permission? | Supplied by the brand owner | Competitor marks; recreating a logo by AI generation |
| Voices | Whose voice? | Stock or preset voices; the user's own voice; a consenting person's voice | Cloning or imitating any real person without consent |
| Real people on screen | Consent? | The user; a consenting artist | Anyone else |
| Generated output | Commercial use allowed on the user's plan? | Check the generation service's terms for the user's plan | Unknown plan terms for a paid client job |

Record each asset and its status in `brief.md`. If something is
unclear, say so plainly and let the user decide; for private, for-friends
videos the bar is lower than for a client or a public brand page, and
say which bar you are applying.
